Board Reporting & Board Assurance for Aged Care Providers

Good governance runs on good information. We help aged care and healthcare governing bodies build board reporting and board assurance they can rely on — evidence, trends and exceptions, not assumptions.

Aged Care Governance

Board reporting is now a statutory governance question

Under the Aged Care Act 2024, registered providers carry a statutory duty to comply with their obligations (section 179), and responsible persons — including directors — carry a personal due diligence duty (section 180) to take reasonable steps to ensure the provider complies. Directors cannot discharge a due diligence duty on information they never see.

The Strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards expect governing bodies to set priorities, monitor performance and drive improvement — and governing bodies must consider reports from their Quality Care Advisory Body. That only works when board reporting is accurate, trended, and arrives on time.

In practice, many board packs are assembled manually from disconnected spreadsheets, present point-in-time numbers with no trend, and leave directors asking the same question every meeting: how do we know? Closing that gap — between what management reports and what the board can evidence — is what board assurance means.

Board Assurance

What strong board assurance looks like

Three characteristics separate board reporting that provides genuine assurance from reporting that simply fills a board pack.

A single source of truth

Every number in the board pack reconciles with the operational system it came from, definitions don't drift between meetings, and one owner is accountable for accuracy.

Trends and exceptions

Metrics are trended over time against targets and benchmarks, exceptions past agreed thresholds are flagged automatically, and emerging risks surface before they become notifiable events.

A closed action loop

Every reported issue has an owner and due date, open actions are tracked to closure, and the board can see whether interventions actually moved the metric.

Common Questions

Board reporting & assurance FAQs

What is board assurance?
Board assurance is the evidence a governing body relies on to be confident that care, compliance and risk are actually being managed as intended — not just reported as managed. An assurance approach maps each key obligation and material risk to a source of evidence, such as audits, quality indicators, trend data, complaints themes and external reviews, so directors can see how they know, rather than relying on management assertion alone.
What should aged care board reporting include?
Aged care board reporting should cover clinical and quality indicators (such as falls, medication safety and pressure injuries), complaints and feedback themes, SIRS reportable incidents and their trends, care minutes performance, Quality Indicator Program results and Star Ratings, workforce and mandatory training metrics, risk register movements, and financial and prudential obligations. Metrics should be trended over time, compared against targets or benchmarks, and flag exceptions so a non-executive director can absorb them quickly.
How does the Aged Care Act 2024 change board reporting?
The Aged Care Act 2024 places a statutory duty on registered providers (section 179) and a personal due diligence duty on responsible persons (section 180), who must take reasonable steps to ensure the provider complies with its obligations. In practice, that due diligence depends on reliable, current information about care, quality and compliance reaching the governing body — and governing bodies must also consider reports from their Quality Care Advisory Body. Board reporting is the mechanism these duties rely on.
Do you only work with aged care providers?
No. Aged care governance is a core focus, but we provide board reporting and board assurance support to hospitals and day procedure services (aligned to the NSQHS Clinical Governance Standard), disability and community services, and boards and executive teams across the broader health and human services sector in Australia and New Zealand.
