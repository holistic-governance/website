# Strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards

> The strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards are seven standards that set out what quality and safe aged care looks like in Australia. They have applied since 1 November 2025 under the Aged Care Act 2024, and the Aged Care Rules 2025 make them legal obligations. They apply to registered providers in three registration categories: personal and care support in the home or community, nursing and transition care, and residential care. Standards 6 and 7 apply to residential care only. The Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission audits providers against them.

## What are the strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards?

The strengthened Quality Standards set the quality requirements for funded aged care services delivered by registered providers, so that older people receive care that is safe, meets their needs and preferences, and upholds their rights.

Each Standard has four parts:

- **Intent** — the overall purpose of the Standard.
- **Expectation statement** — what the older person can expect from their provider.
- **Outcomes** — what providers are assessed against.
- **Actions** — what providers can do to meet each outcome.

The outcomes are the legal obligations. They are written into Part 6 of the Aged Care Rules 2025. The actions sit in the Department of Health, Disability and Ageing's Standards document and in Commission guidance.

They replaced the previous Aged Care Quality Standards (in effect 1 July 2019 to 31 October 2025) and are "more detailed and measurable", according to the Commission. The review followed the Royal Commission into Aged Care Quality and Safety, which found gaps in areas including dementia care, diversity, provider governance, food and nutrition, and clinical care.

## What is the statutory basis?

- **Aged Care Act 2024 (Cth)** — section 15 allows the rules to prescribe quality standards and to apply them by registration category or kind of provider. Under section 146, conforming with the Standards is a condition of registration for registered providers of a kind prescribed by the rules.
- **Aged Care Rules 2025 (F2025L01173)** — Part 6 sets out the seven Standards and their outcomes (sections 15-10 to 15-40), and section 15-5 says which registration categories each Standard applies to. Section 146-5 prescribes which providers must comply.
- The **Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission Act 2018** was repealed on 1 November 2025.

## Who do the Standards apply to?

Under the Act, every provider of government-funded aged care is a registered provider, registered in one or more registration categories. The Standards apply according to category:

| Category | Standards that apply |
|---|---|
| 1–3: home and community services; assistive technology and home modifications; advisory and support services | None. These providers must still uphold the Statement of Rights, follow the Aged Care Code of Conduct and report serious incidents |
| 4: personal and care support in the home or community | Standards 1 to 4. Outcome 5.1 (Clinical governance) if the provider delivers care management or restorative care management |
| 5: nursing and transition care | Standards 1 to 5 |
| 6: residential care | All seven Standards |

**Support at Home.** Under the single provider model, a provider claiming for Support at Home services must be registered in category 4 with the care management service type, be audited against the Standards, and meet Outcome 5.1.

**Subscription trial category (from 1 July 2026).** An amendment to the Rules added a provider registration category called subscription trial. A provider in that category must meet Standards 1 to 4 only if it is also registered in category 4 or 5, and Standard 5 only if it is also registered in category 5. This is the only amendment to Part 6 of the Rules since it commenced. The Standards and their outcomes have not been amended.

## What are the key obligations?

- **Governing body accountability** — "The governing body is accountable for the delivery of quality funded aged care services and must maintain oversight of all aspects of the provider's operations" (Outcome 2.3).
- **Partnering with older people** — meaningful and active partnerships with older people to inform organisational priorities and continuous improvement (Outcome 2.1).
- **Quality, risk and incident systems** — a quality system that drives continuous improvement, plus risk and incident management systems (Outcomes 2.3 to 2.5).
- **Complaints and feedback** — aged care workers and older people are supported to complain without reprisal, and complaints drive improvement (Outcomes 2.6a–b).
- **Workforce** — planning and human resource management so workers are competent and skilled (Outcomes 2.8 and 2.9).
- **Clinical governance** — the governing body must ensure the provider continuously improves the safety and quality of clinical care, and clinical governance must be integrated into corporate governance (Outcome 5.1).
- **Communication and deterioration** — risks and changes or deterioration in an older person's condition are escalated and communicated (Outcome 3.3).
- **Restrictive practices** — minimised and, where used, a last resort, least restrictive, for the shortest time, with informed consent, and monitored and reviewed (action 3.2.7). Detailed requirements are in Part 7 of the Rules.
- **Supporters** — supporters of older people are involved in care and are informed and supported, including during the last days of life (Standards 3 and 5).
- **Continuous improvement** — every registered provider must show capability for, and commitment to, continuous improvement. Providers in categories 4, 5 and 6 must also have a continuous improvement plan (Act section 147; Rules sections 147-5 and 147-10).

## How are providers audited and assessed?

The Commission audits against the Standards when a provider applies to register, renew or vary its registration in category 4, 5 or 6. Audits focus on governance, systems and processes for delivering care, the experience and outcomes for older people, and whether practices are safe across services. The Commission meets the governing body and senior management, then tests whether systems are embedded in care delivery. It visits each category 6 service and some category 4 and 5 services.

Each outcome is rated **conformance**, **minor non-conformance** or **major non-conformance**, and the rating for each Standard is based on its outcome ratings. Providers renewing registration in category 6 can also receive an **exceeding** rating. The Commission responds to non-conformance in proportion to the risk to older people, and publishes audit results on its "Find a report" page.

## What is the implementation timeline?

- **2021** — the Royal Commission into Aged Care Quality and Safety recommended an urgent review of the Quality Standards (Recommendations 18 to 21).
- **February 2025** — an earlier version of the strengthened Standards was released. The final version made minor changes to it.
- **August 2025** — final Standards published by the Department, reflecting the final draft of the Aged Care Rules released on 31 July 2025.
- **24 September 2025** — Aged Care Rules 2025 registered.
- **1 November 2025** — the Aged Care Act 2024, the Rules and the strengthened Standards commenced. The 2019 Standards ended on 31 October 2025.
- **1 July 2026** — subscription trial category added, with its own Standards application rules.
- **August 2026** — the Department posted an updated copy of its Standards document, titled "Strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards – November 2025". The Standards in the Rules were not amended, so this is not a new set of Standards.

The Commission says the government will review the Standards every 5 years.

## What are common provider gaps?

From HG's work with providers:

- **Thin outcomes evidence** — policies and processes exist, but little evidence of the outcome for the older person.
- **Standard 2 underestimated** — governing bodies have not changed their oversight to show they are accountable for "all aspects" of operations.
- **Clinical governance not integrated** — a framework exists on paper but is not part of corporate governance or board reporting.
- **Category 4 providers missing Outcome 5.1** — home care providers delivering care management assume Standard 5 does not apply to them.
- **No continuous improvement plan** — or a plan that is not linked to feedback, complaints, incidents and audit findings.
- **Partnering is consultation only** — older people give views but do not shape priorities.
- **Restrictive practices** — use is not minimised, consented to, monitored and reviewed in line with action 3.2.7 and Part 7 of the Rules.

## Glossary

- **Registered provider** — an entity registered by the Commissioner to deliver funded aged care services, in one or more registration categories.
- **Registration category** — a group of service types that sets a provider's obligations. There are six numbered categories, plus subscription trial from 1 July 2026.
- **Outcome** — what providers are assessed against; a legal obligation under the Rules.
- **Supporter** — a person registered under section 37 of the Act who, with the older person's consent, supports them while maintaining their ability to make their own decisions.
