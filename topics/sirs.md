# Serious Incident Response Scheme (SIRS)

> The Serious Incident Response Scheme (SIRS) is the mandatory framework for preventing, managing, notifying and learning from serious incidents in Australian aged care. It is administered by the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission. Since 1 November 2025 it operates under the Aged Care Act 2024 and the Aged Care Rules 2025, which narrowed the Priority 1 test, redefined neglect and merged two incident types into "unexplained absence".

## What is the Serious Incident Response Scheme (SIRS)?

SIRS requires registered providers to:

1. **Assess** every incident to decide whether it is a reportable incident, and if so whether it is Priority 1 or Priority 2.
2. **Notify** the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission of reportable incidents within the required timeframes, through the My Aged Care provider portal.
3. **Manage** every incident, reportable or not, through an incident management system (IMS): keep the older person safe, involve the relevant people, and use an open disclosure process.
4. **Learn and prevent** by collecting and reviewing incident data, asking whether incidents could have been prevented, and acting to stop them recurring.

SIRS is not only a reporting regime. The Commission expects the incident management system to be operating continuously, and providers must be able to give IMS records to the Commission on request.

## What is the statutory basis?

- **Aged Care Act 2024 (Cth)** — section 16 defines a reportable incident and lists the eight incident types. The Act also protects people who disclose reportable incidents, including aged care workers, older people, supporters, volunteers and advocates.
- **Aged Care Rules 2025** — definitions and clarifications of the incident types, the Priority 1 and Priority 2 tests, notification timeframes, and incident management requirements.
- The **Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission Act 2018** was repealed on 1 November 2025. The Commission's functions and powers now sit in the Aged Care Act 2024.

## Who does SIRS apply to?

SIRS applies to registered providers, for reportable incidents that occur, are alleged to have occurred, or are suspected of having occurred in connection with the delivery of funded aged care services (s 16). It started in residential care on 1 April 2021 and was extended to home services on 1 December 2022. For services in home or community settings, providers must notify all reportable incidents connected with care.

## What are the reportable incident types?

There are eight reportable incident types (s 16(1)):

1. **Unreasonable use of force** against the older person.
2. **Unlawful sexual contact or inappropriate sexual conduct** inflicted on the older person.
3. **Psychological or emotional abuse** of the older person.
4. **Unexpected death** of the older person.
5. **Stealing or financial coercion** of the older person by an aged care worker of the provider.
6. **Neglect** of the older person. Since 1 November 2025 this covers a registered provider, aged care worker or responsible person who:
   - delivered aged care services that exposed the older person to risk of serious injury or illness; or
   - was reckless or intentionally negligent in delivering services; or
   - caused or contributed to a significant failure to deliver services, or to a systemic (repeated or ongoing) pattern of poor conduct; or
   - delivered a grossly inadequate service.
7. **Inappropriate use of restrictive practices** — use of a restrictive practice other than in accordance with the requirements in the Rules.
8. **Unexplained absence** of the older person in the course of the delivery of funded aged care services. Since 1 November 2025 this single type replaces both "missing consumer" (home and community settings) and "unexplained absence" (residential care homes).

Providers must report incidents that are suspected or alleged even if they cannot be confirmed, incidents already under internal or police investigation, and incidents involving people who do not want them reported. Permission from the people involved is not needed.

## What are the priority levels and notification timeframes?

**Priority 1** is any reportable incident:

- that has caused the older person physical or psychological injury or discomfort that requires medical or psychological treatment to resolve; or
- where there are reasonable grounds to report it to the police; or
- involving unlawful sexual contact or inappropriate sexual conduct inflicted on the older person; or
- involving unexpected death resulting from care services delivered, or not delivered, by the provider; or
- involving unexplained absence where there are reasonable grounds to call the police.

Notify the Commission **within 24 hours** of becoming aware, and give any information not available at the time **within the following 5 days**. Where there are reasonable grounds, the police must also be notified within 24 hours.

**Priority 2** is any other reportable incident, including an incident, allegation or suspicion that could reasonably have been expected to cause harm. Notify the Commission **within 30 calendar days** of becoming aware.

For both priorities, notify the Commission of any significant new information as soon as reasonably practical.

**What changed on 1 November 2025:**

- Priority 1 no longer includes incidents that "could reasonably have been expected to have caused" harm. Providers assess the actual impact, using a person-centred, culturally informed and trauma-informed approach, rather than predicting a worst case. Potential harm now sits in Priority 2.
- "Medical or psychological treatment" means treatment that only a medical practitioner, nurse practitioner, registered nurse, psychologist or social worker can provide. General treatment that anyone can provide does not make an incident Priority 1, unless another Priority 1 criterion applies.
- A person who cannot communicate how an incident affected them may still have been affected. Providers should assess impact over time, for example through changes in behaviour such as withdrawal or fear of certain care or people. The Commission publishes an Impact Assessment Tool for this.

## What are the incident management system obligations?

Providers must create and keep an effective incident management system and document its procedures. For every incident, reportable or not, the system must support:

- **Assessment** — whether the incident is reportable and its priority, what response is needed, and who must be involved or notified, including the older person and their supporters.
- **Response** — action to keep the people affected safe, healthy and well, and to resolve the situation.
- **Notification** — to the Commission, and to the police where there are reasonable grounds.
- **Open disclosure** with the older person and their supporters.
- **Learning** — collecting and reviewing incident data to monitor how well risks are managed, whether incidents could have been prevented, and how to stop further harm.
- **Prevention** — risk management systems and practices that stop incidents from happening.
- **Records** — available to the Commission on request.

## How SIRS interacts with the Strengthened Standards

SIRS sits inside the Strengthened Aged Care Quality Standards, which apply from 1 November 2025:

- **Standard 2 (The Organisation)** — incident management is part of organisational governance, risk and quality systems.
- **Standard 3 (Care and Services)** — incidents arising from care planning and delivery, transitions and communication.
- **Standard 5 (Clinical Care)** — clinical incidents, including deterioration, medication, falls, pressure injuries and restrictive practices.
- **Standard 6 (Food and Nutrition)** — incidents involving choking, food safety or inadequate nutrition.
- **Standard 7 (The Residential Community)** — incidents in residential care homes, including unexplained absence and incidents between residents.

The Commission can ask for incident management system records at any time.

## What are common provider gaps?

- **Still applying the old Priority 1 test** — rating incidents Priority 1 because of potential harm, or using first aid alone as the trigger, after the 1 November 2025 changes.
- **Inconsistent identification** — workers do not consistently recognise reportable incidents, especially psychological abuse, neglect under its new definition, and inappropriate restrictive practices.
- **Impact not assessed for people who cannot communicate it** — no follow-up assessment over time for people with cognitive impairment.
- **Missed 5-day follow-up** — information not available at the first notification is not sent within 5 days.
- **Investigation depth** — investigations stop at the immediate event, without root-cause analysis or systemic review.
- **Action tracking gaps** — actions are assigned but not tracked to closure or verified.
- **Restrictive practices** — chemical restraint not recognised as a possible reportable incident.
- **Supporters not involved** — families and other supporters are told about an incident but not supported through open disclosure.
- **Board oversight** — incident data reaches executives, but the board does not see themes, notification performance or system-level improvement.
- **Worker support** — workers involved in serious incidents do not get consistent support.

## Glossary

- **Reportable incident** — one of the eight incident types in s 16 of the Aged Care Act 2024 that has occurred, is alleged or is suspected, in connection with the delivery of funded aged care services.
- **Priority 1** — a reportable incident that has caused injury or discomfort needing medical or psychological treatment to resolve, or with reasonable grounds to report to police, or involving sexual contact or conduct, unexpected death resulting from care, or unexplained absence with grounds to call police. Notify within 24 hours.
- **Priority 2** — any other reportable incident, including one that could reasonably have been expected to cause harm. Notify within 30 calendar days.
- **Medical or psychological treatment** — treatment that only a medical practitioner, nurse practitioner, registered nurse, psychologist or social worker can provide.
- **Unexplained absence** — the single incident type, from 1 November 2025, for an older person's unexplained absence during the delivery of care in any setting.
- **Restrictive practice** — any practice or intervention that has the effect of restricting the rights or freedom of movement of an older person (s 17).
